Thomas Hobbes was a major seventeenth-century English political philosopher and mechanistic thinker. Living through civil war and revolution, he long asked how a stable public authority could be rebuilt amid conflict.

In *Leviathan* he offers the famous thought experiment of the state of nature: without a common authority that can settle disputes and enforce rules, competition, diffidence, and glory may leave people in lasting insecurity. For safety, people covenant to entrust some power to a public authority; the state becomes an institution for order.

Hobbes insists the sovereign must have force enough to make rules, judge disputes, and keep the peace. His argument places security at the center of modern state theory, and leaves a question later writers keep asking: when state power grows large, who constrains the sovereign itself.
