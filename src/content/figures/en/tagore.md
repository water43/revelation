Rabindranath Tagore stands at the center of modern Indian letters. Writing primarily in Bengali and later reaching the world through English translations, he carried a South Asian lyric tradition into the early twentieth-century global imagination.

His poetry often turns on nature, the divine, and human love, in language that is clear and musical. The English rendering of *Gitanjali* brought him the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1913—the first awarded to an Asian writer.

Beyond verse, Tagore wrote fiction, drama, and songs, and founded Visva-Bharati University as a meeting ground of East and West. His name has become nearly synonymous with a gentle, expansive poetic spirit.
