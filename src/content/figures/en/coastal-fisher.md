The coastal fisher is the “capture” branch of fishery: nearshore nets, hooks, and traps for wild fish, shrimp, crab, and shellfish—not a single biography. In many village traditions men go out with the boat; only after landing does the catch enter the Newhaven fishwife’s haul-and-sale circuit. What is taken is a commons, not a private pond asset—so overfishing, fuel, and weather push risk to the bow, not to a pen.

Here fish, shrimp, and crab are catch species, not culture stock. Tide sense, ground knowledge, and crew coordination are the core skills. Information stays oral: where the fish are, which day the wind rises, which buyer short-weighs. Without landing records and vessel tracks, experience walks with the old helmsman, and ground knowledge scatters.

Against the pond aquaculturist: one manages a private water crop; the other contests a common pool. Against the fishwife: one takes from the waves; the other sells on the road.

## Historical limits

The constraint is structural. With open access, the rational household sets one more net; the collective result is a thinner ground. Safety and weather warning were historically weak; one gale can take a boat. Fuel, gear, and quay handling bind fishers to merchant credit. Oral ground lore does not settle into enforceable quotas or closed-season science.

## Personal and role limits

These are limits of the type. Skill is bound to reading tide, helm, mending nets, and stamina. That does not convert easily into stock assessment or cold-chain plant management. Spare capital is thin; repair and accident can empty a household. In this gallery the pond keeper can still change density; the catcher cannot change how much the sea still holds.

## A modern path

The point is not endless nets. It is to turn the commons into governed quotas and traceable landings.

Safety and weather first: inspection, life-saving gear, and alerts down to the boat. Traceable landings: species, weight, ground, and time on the books for inspection and market. Quotas held by cooperatives: shared fuel and ice, less distress undercutting; information kept with crew and producer organizations instead of nameless quay supply.
