Thomas Stearns Eliot was a defining renovator of twentieth-century English poetry. Early works such as “The Love Song of J. Alfred Prufrock” and *The Waste Land* gave voice to urban anxiety, cultural fragmentation, and crisis of belief, bringing allusion, polyglot texture, and montage into modern verse.

Later poems such as *Four Quartets* turn toward time, redemption, and contemplation—more inward in tone, still dense with music and thought. He was also a major critic and dramatist; his essay on tradition and the individual talent remains foundational.

Eliot received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1948. His poetry and criticism helped shape modernism’s self-understanding: how, in a broken age, to keep speaking with the voices of the past.
