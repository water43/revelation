Nietzsche wrote in aphorism, poetry, and polemic, attacking the “slave morality” shaped by traditional metaphysics and Christian ethics. “God is dead” is less a simple atheist slogan than a diagnosis of the collapse of Europe’s value foundations.

Will to power, the overman, and eternal recurrence aim to imagine, beyond the abyss of nihilism, a yes-saying creation of new values. His genealogies of truth, ascetic ideals, and *ressentiment* opened paths later taken by critical theory and existentialism.

His influence reaches far beyond academic philosophy: Rilke, Heidegger, Foucault, Deleuze, and countless modernists drew fire—or fought back—from his pages.
