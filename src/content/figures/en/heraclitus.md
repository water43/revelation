Heraclitus of Ephesus is a Pre-Socratic thinker whose work survives only in fragments, yet shaped later ideas of change, opposition, and *logos*.

He held that all things are in process, and that opposites depend on and turn into one another. Fire often serves as his image of cosmic process: consuming, transforming, and yet enduring through change.

Obscure and oracular in style, Heraclitus remains a source of the Western tradition of becoming, set in sharp contrast to Parmenides’ insistence on the permanence of Being.
