John Locke was a pivotal seventeenth-century English thinker in empiricism and political philosophy. He asked how knowledge arises from experience, and why government must be limited.

In the *Two Treatises of Government* he holds that people already possess basic rights to life, liberty, and property in the state of nature. Government is formed to supply common law, impartial judgment, and executive force, the better to protect those rights. Its power comes from popular authorization and must be bound by law and institutions; if it betrays that end and violates basic rights, the people may resist and even replace it.

Locke recasts the state from an authority that merely demands obedience into a public body accountable to the people—shaping later ideas of constitutionalism, bills of rights, and limited government.
