This essay sits under the Master Nutritionist entry. It shows how nutrition principles become **a week of three meals**. It is not a clinical prescription and cannot replace individual medical advice. With illness, pregnancy, a history of eating disorders, or special medication, coordinate first with a physician and a credentialed nutrition professional. The default reader here is an adult in mostly urban / office life, light-to-moderate daily activity, and no special medical restrictions.

## 1. Lock the structure, then pick dishes

High-level practice usually fixes structure before dish names:

- **Breakfast**: protein + whole grain or tuber + fruit/veg; skip sugary drinks as the base.
- **Lunch**: about a third of the day’s energy; half a plate non-starchy vegetables + a palm of quality protein + a fist of staple carbohydrate.
- **Dinner**: may run a little lighter than lunch; protein and vegetables remain; staples rise or fall with activity.
- **Fluids**: water and unsweetened tea first; alcohol and sugary drinks are not part of the health floor.
- **Swaps**: stay inside groups (dark leafy ↔ gourds; fish ↔ poultry ↔ soy; brown rice ↔ oats ↔ whole wheat) so the plan stays executable.

Daily kilocalorie precision matters less than a **stable weekly structure**.

## 2. A sample week (breakfast / lunch / dinner)

### Monday
- **Breakfast**: Greek or unsweetened yogurt, oats, berries, a few nuts.
- **Lunch**: brown rice, steamed fish, broccoli and carrot, light tomato-egg soup.
- **Dinner**: mixed-grain porridge or a little whole-wheat noodles, mushroom chicken breast, cucumber salad.

### Tuesday
- **Breakfast**: whole-wheat toast, eggs, thin avocado, unsweetened soy milk.
- **Lunch**: buckwheat noodles, shrimp with seasonal vegetables, wood-ear and tofu.
- **Dinner**: sweet potato, roast chicken leg (skin off), stir-fried greens.

### Wednesday
- **Breakfast**: millet porridge, boiled egg, spinach salad, a small handful of soybeans.
- **Lunch**: quinoa bowl, beef with peppers, garlic lettuce.
- **Dinner**: vegetable-tofu noodle soup (light oil), seaweed salad.

### Thursday
- **Breakfast**: overnight oats (milk or unsweetened plant milk), diced apple, flaxseed.
- **Lunch**: mixed-grain rice, steamed sea bass, greens in light broth.
- **Dinner**: roast pumpkin, shrimp-tofu clay pot, pea shoots.

### Friday
- **Breakfast**: whole-wheat flatbread, low-fat cheese or soft tofu (go easy on salt), orange.
- **Lunch**: whole-wheat pasta, tomato-tuna sauce, large mixed salad.
- **Dinner**: corn, sliced poached chicken breast, stir-fried leafy greens.

### Saturday (room for social meals)
- **Breakfast**: egg-and-veg wrap in a whole-wheat tortilla, unsweetened yogurt.
- **Lunch**: when eating out—steamed/baked fish or poached shrimp, an extra vegetable side, stop the staple at about 70% full.
- **Dinner**: home-style tofu clay pot, garlic broccoli, a small serving of rice.

### Sunday (set up the next week)
- **Breakfast**: oat omelette, tomato, a small glass of milk.
- **Lunch**: chicken-breast salad bowl (mixed greens, legumes, oil-and-vinegar), one slice whole-wheat bread.
- **Dinner**: light hot-pot style: plenty of greens and mushrooms, fish slices or shrimp balls, a little wide noodle.

## 3. Common corrections in real life

- **Hungry too soon**: raise protein and vegetable volume first, then staple—not sweet drinks first.
- **A party blows the day**: return to structure at the next meals; no punitive starvation.
- **Constipation or bloating**: raise fiber gradually and keep fluids up; a sudden jump in roughage often ends the plan.
- **Training days**: nudge staples and protein around the session; rest days need not copy training portions.

## 4. What the nutritionist actually delivers

Dish names can change; **structure cannot**: protein at each meal, vegetable fiber present, staples floating with activity, flavors rotated across the week so boredom does not kill adherence. The master nutritionist’s job is to make this table runnable for seven real days—and to translate panic, trends, and bans back into a repeatable everyday practice.
